The Lake Floor under Northwest Ohio

Northwest Ohio sits on the bed of glacial lakes that drained away about 14,000 to 12,000 years ago. Their old shorelines survive as sand beach ridges only a few meters high. The flat lake bed between them became the Great Black Swamp.

Elevation comes from AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen terrarium, zoom 10, about 115 m per pixel, derived mainly from USGS 3DEP). Local relief is each cell's height minus the average height within about 1 km. It brings out beach ridges and stream cuts that a normal color ramp hides. Lake-stage elevations are approximate. The real shorelines tilt upward to the northeast because the land rose after the ice melted (isostatic rebound).
